Before you flew

A rainy night, the car in the dark, a watch on your wrist, your face, and a tag with my name on it. Five moments I keep going back to.

a rainy night · the first photo of us

The two of us

It was raining, and we were together. Somewhere in that night, in front of a mirror, both in black and both grinning, we took our first photo together.

It isn't a perfect picture. It doesn't need to be. It's proof of two people being completely happy in the same place at the same time, and I look at it more than I'll ever admit.

We made memories that night that I still carry everywhere. Now every time it rains, I'm back in that mirror with you.

The first photo of us, and still the one I go back to.

Somewhere in the dark, your hand in mine.

19 · 09 · 2026 · two nights before you flew

The nineteenth of September

Two nights before you flew. The car, the dark, and your hand in mine.

At the time it just felt like holding on. Looking back, I understand what it was: one of the last nights I could reach across and find you right there, close enough to touch.

That's why one of the counters at the end of this page starts from the nineteenth. I wanted to measure the distance from a night I was holding your hand, not only from the morning I had to let it go.

Your hand in mine, in the car, in the dark. That was enough.

the gift · a watch and a thin silver chain

Something of mine, on your wrist

I gave you a watch and a thin silver chain. Nothing grand. I just wanted you to have something of mine you could actually touch, in a place I couldn't follow.

And you wear them, both of them. I can't really explain what that does to me. Knowing they're on your wrist in Manchester right now is one of the quiet things that gets me through a day.

Whatever time that watch shows over there, a few hours behind mine, it's still counting from me.

Every time you check the time over there, that's me.

you · glasses on

This face

The face I picture every time your name lights up my phone.

Glasses on, half a smile, sitting in the car. This is the face I'll be waiting to see again.

People over there are going to meet you and think they're meeting someone new. They'll really be meeting the person who wrote to me “I will come… no matter what” and meant every word.

Look after that face for me. Wear the glasses, get some sleep, and let it smile properly when something good happens.

My letter to you

Shayan,

You wrote that you can't even say it in words. I understand that now. I'm going to try anyway, because you deserve to have it written down somewhere you can always come back to.

I still go back to that rainy night. The mirror, the two of us in black, both grinning. I didn't know then that it would become the photo I'd look at most. And the nineteenth of September, in the car in the dark, your hand in mine, two nights before you flew. I think part of me knew even then that I was holding something I'd have to let go of for a while. Not lose. Let go of, for a while. There's a difference, and I hold on to it every day.

Then came the black box. The watch with its silver case and black dial. The fine chain with the little peach stone. The card with my name on it. And your letter, covered in red hearts. You wrote that I'm an angel for you. You wrote that if I ever need you for anything, you will come, no matter what. You wrote that you're going to miss me so much I can't even imagine. I read it and I cried, and I'm not embarrassed to say so. I have never felt so loved by anyone.

On the twenty-first I watched you go, one flight after another: SV739 to Jeddah, then SV123 over France, a little green plane on my screen, and your updates keeping me company the whole way. When you landed in Manchester at 12:55, I said Alhamdulillah and finally breathed.

So here is what I need you to know.

I am so proud of you. It takes real courage to pack a dark grey suitcase and start a life somewhere new. You carried my name on that tag. I carry yours too: in my head, in my day, in every dua I make.

Wear the watch. Keep the chain on. On the days Manchester feels too big or too grey, look at your wrist and remember there is someone in Lahore, a few hours ahead of you, always the first to reach tomorrow and saving you a place in it.

Eat properly. Sleep. Call me when you can't. Tell me the small things, especially the small things. And if something ever gets too heavy, give some of it to me. That's what I'm here for.

May Allah keep you safe, make this new life easy for you, and bring us back into the same room, whenever He writes it. Until then, I'll be here, under the same sky, looking up.

Always yours, and always loving you back,

DIRE WOLF ♥︎
